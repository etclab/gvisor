RFC 8446 specifies version 1.3 of the Transport Layer Security (TLS) protocol, which secures client/server communications over the Internet by providing authentication, confidentiality, and integrity. It obsoletes RFCs 5077, 5246, and 6961, and updates RFCs 5705 and 6066, while also introducing new requirements for TLS 1.2 implementations. TLS 1.3 brings major improvements over TLS 1.2, including removal of legacy and static key-exchange cipher suites in favor of forward-secret (EC)DHE exchanges, encryption of nearly all handshake messages after the ServerHello, and a redesigned HKDF-based key schedule. It also introduces a 0-RTT mode for reduced connection latency, a unified PSK-based resumption mechanism, and a simplified, more consistent handshake state machine. The document is organized into sections covering the handshake protocol, record protocol, cryptographic computations, 0-RTT/anti-replay considerations, compliance requirements, and extensive appendices detailing state machines, data structures, backward compatibility, and security properties.
